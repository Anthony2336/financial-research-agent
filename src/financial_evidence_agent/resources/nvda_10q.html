NVIDIA Corporation

Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Data center revenue grew as cloud service providers, consumer internet companies, and enterprise customers deployed accelerated computing systems for artificial intelligence workloads. Demand for our data center platforms reflected purchases of compute, networking, and software that customers use to train and operate generative AI models. Revenue growth also reflected customer adoption of full-rack systems and the transition toward data center architectures that combine GPUs, CPUs, networking, and software. We believe customers continue to evaluate their infrastructure plans based on available power, facility capacity, deployment schedules, and the pace at which new products become available. Our data center business is affected by the timing of customer acceptance, supply availability, product mix, and the ability of customers to complete installation and commissioning. Large deployments may be recognized over several periods because delivery, installation, and acceptance can occur at different times. The scale of individual data center projects can cause quarterly revenue to vary, even when long-term demand remains strong. We continue to work with suppliers and manufacturing partners to increase capacity while maintaining product quality and reliability. Our networking products are increasingly purchased with compute systems because customers seek to reduce communication bottlenecks among accelerated servers. Enterprise demand includes customers modernizing information technology infrastructure, developing AI applications, and using accelerated computing for scientific and industrial workloads. Consumer internet companies continue to invest in recommendation, search, advertising, and generative AI services that require substantial computing resources. Cloud service providers may purchase capacity for their own services and for resale to enterprise customers. The pace of revenue growth depends on customer capital spending, the availability of power and data center space, competitive offerings, export controls, and our ability to supply products in required configurations. Management monitors orders, supply commitments, customer deployment schedules, and channel inventory, but those indicators may not predict future revenue with precision. We expect demand for accelerated computing to remain an important driver of data center revenue, although the timing and magnitude of customer purchases can change. Our results also depend on the mix of products sold, including systems, networking products, and software-related offerings. Changes in product mix, customer concentration, geographic demand, and foreign currency rates can affect revenue and gross margin. We evaluate these factors as part of our planning process and update our operating plans when conditions change. The discussion in this filing is based on information available as of the filing date and should be considered together with the risk factors described below.

Risk Factors

Our revenue concentration among a limited number of customers and the size of their individual purchases may cause our operating results to fluctuate. Customers may delay, reduce, cancel, or reschedule data center orders due to changes in their capital spending plans, available financing, power constraints, data center construction delays, or changes in demand for their own products and services. Demand for accelerated computing may not develop as expected, and investments in artificial intelligence infrastructure may not produce the returns customers expect. If customers reduce their spending, our revenue, gross margin, and cash flows could be adversely affected. We rely on a complex global supply chain for components, assembly, testing, and logistics. Supply constraints, manufacturing interruptions, component shortages, quality issues, transportation disruptions, or a failure by suppliers to meet commitments could limit our ability to deliver products on time. Our products are subject to intense competition from companies with substantial resources and from customers that develop their own hardware or software solutions. Competitors may introduce products with better performance, lower prices, broader ecosystems, or different technical approaches. Government actions, including export controls, sanctions, tariffs, licensing requirements, and other trade restrictions, may limit our ability to sell products in certain markets or require changes to product designs and distribution arrangements. Changes in regulations may create uncertainty for customers and channel partners. Our future results depend on our ability to develop and introduce products on schedule, support complex system deployments, attract and retain employees, protect intellectual property, and manage cybersecurity risks. A disruption involving security, privacy, or the availability of our systems could harm customer confidence and result in significant costs. The timing of product transitions can create inventory, pricing, and demand risks. If demand forecasts prove inaccurate, we may have excess inventory, insufficient supply, or commitments that reduce profitability. The matters described in these risk factors could materially affect our business, financial condition, operating results, and cash flows.

Our estimates, plans, and expectations are subject to substantial uncertainty and may change as market conditions develop. We may incur additional costs to respond to customer requirements, regulatory developments, or changes in the competitive environment. A prolonged mismatch between demand, supply, and deployment capacity could adversely affect future results.
